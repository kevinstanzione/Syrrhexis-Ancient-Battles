Unit Type

Every profile in the game has a Unit Type, which indicates to the player if the model is a Warrior on foot, a mounted Cavalry, soldiers fighting on a Chariot or Elephant, or some sort of Warmachine. Each Unit Type has special rules found here - Unit Types.

Move

This is the value in inches that a model can move during its activation. Quicker units can move further, but bulky armor and equipment can bring Move value down. The average move distance is 5”. The Move printed on a profile is before any penalties from Gear, Equipment or special rules such as Phalanx; apply those on top. See Movement.

Training

From levied peasants to elite professional soldiers, soldiers in the ages past fought with a large diversity of training and tactical know-how. Training affects a fighter's ability to remain on the battlefield under duress, withstand terrifying charges, and perform complicated strategies. See Psychology for how Training affects soldier psychology, and Pull for a Melee move that depends on Training.

Points

Armies are evaluated by their total points, with standard games at 2000 points per side, or 1250 points per player in 2v2 games. Each unit is composed of many warriors who each cost some amount of points, with the sum known as the Unit Total. A Warmachine unit’s profile lists its war engine’s cost in brackets, for example “1 Warmachine [50] & 3–6 Engineers”: the unit costs the war engine’s points plus the profile’s points for each Engineer. See Building an Army for more details.

Gear, Equipment, and Upgrades

Each model in a unit must have identical Loadout - which refers to the combination of Gear and Equipment the model uses. This means that if a player building an army wishes to take an Upgrade, such as taking additional armor, they must give that upgrade to every model in the unit, unless otherwise noted.

All weapons a model is equipped with are stated in the Gear and Equipment of the fighter. Equipment must be physically present on the model, while Gear need not be actually identifiable physically on the model. Special Characters may ignore the requirement for Equipment to be physically shown, and instead should be specially identifiable as a Special Character of some sort.

Unit Composition

The unit will have one or more allowable compositions when fielded in battle, listing the number of ranks and files the soldiers fight in if it is a Closed Order unit. For example, 2 Ranks of 5 warriors mean a tray of 10 models, in 2 ranks of 5 figurines. A unit can only be fielded in these Compositions, unless otherwise stated by special rules or a scenario rule. For Open Order units, any number of models in the specified range may be taken.

Special Characters

Similar to Upgrades, a number of options for upgrading models to Special Characters will usually be available to a unit. There are two types of Special Character - general and Retinue.

General special characters can be fielded in most typical units, while Retinue Special Characters can typically only be fielded in special Retinue units. Additionally, Retinue Special Characters increase your Initiative, and only one of each kind can be taken per army, barring special scenarios. The exact rules for Special Characters can be found here - Special Characters.

Strength

This value governs a fighter’s ability to batter foes in melee combat. Each weapon a fighter uses has its own Strength value, which might not be the same even for the same weapon on another profile. See Melee to see how Strength affects Wounding Rolls, and see Gear and Equipment to learn more about Equipment that require high Strength.

Accuracy

This characteristic demonstrates a soldier’s lithe and accurate nature : it lets them land blows in combat and hit targets with ranged weaponry. Each weapon a fighter uses has its own Accuracy value, which might not be the same even for the same weapon on another profile. See Melee and Shooting to learn how attacks are affected by Accuracy.

Physical Aspects of a Model

Each miniature in Syrrhēxis: Ancient Battles represents a single fighting man, making this game more representative of moderately sized skirmishes, or an abstraction of what a larger fight could be. This game does not seek to emulate battles with thousands of men per side.
