c. 225 BC–AD 9 · From Telamon to the Teutoburg Forest

To the Greeks and Romans, everything north of the Alps was barbarian country. Celtic peoples held most of it, from Britain and Gaul to the Danube, and their warbands had sacked Rome itself in 390 BC. At Telamon in 225 BC the Gaesatae, hired warriors from beyond the Alps, fought naked in the front rank against two Roman armies. Further north lived the Germanic peoples. The Cimbri and Teutones came south with their wagons and families and destroyed Roman armies before Marius finally beat them in 102 and 101 BC. Caesar spent eight years conquering Gaul, crossed into Britain, and fought Ariovistus' Germans on the Rhine. Rome never conquered Germania: in AD 9 Arminius led three legions into the Teutoburg Forest, and almost none came out.

These were armies of free warriors, not drilled soldiers. A Gallic chief rode to war with his sworn retainers, the ambacti, while the tribe followed on foot with spear and shield to the blare of the carnyx. The wealthy wore mail, which the Gauls are often credited with inventing, and fought with long slashing swords. The Britons still drove chariots, dropping their warriors at the enemy's line. The Germans carried the framea, a spear that could be thrust or thrown, and a chieftain's companions swore never to leave a battle alive if he fell. Picked young runners fought among the horsemen, keeping pace by holding the horses' manes. Romans wrote that the first charge of these armies was terrifying, and that they tired if it failed.

On the table, the European Barbarians list is built around that first charge. Every Celtic and Germanic unit begins with a Furor token, which hits hard when it charges and costs it Training afterwards, so pick your moment and strike before the enemy can strike you. Each unit is either Celtic or Germanic (the Agrestēs levy is neither), and some Command cards care which: Essedon needs an all-Celtic army, Teutoburg an all-Germanic one, the Druidic Curse a Celtic unit and the Barditus a Germanic unit with a Musician. Mix the two peoples for a wider choice of units, or keep the army pure for its strongest card.
