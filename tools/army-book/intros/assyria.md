c. 900–539 BC · From Ashurnasirpal II to the fall of Babylon

From their heartland on the upper Tigris, the kings of Aššur built the first great empire of the Iron Age. Ashurnasirpal II and Shalmaneser III campaigned every year, and Tiglath-Pileser III turned seasonal musters into a standing army of professional soldiers, paid and equipped by the palace. Under Sargon II, Sennacherib, Esarhaddon and Ashurbanipal, Assyria ruled from the Zagros to the Nile, storming Lachish, sacking Babylon and driving the Kushite pharaohs from Egypt. When Nineveh fell in 612 BC, the Neo-Babylonian kings took up the same weapons and the same way of war, until Cyrus the Great entered Babylon in 539 BC.

The palace reliefs show this army in remarkable detail. Spearmen in lamellar shirts and pointed helmets held the line, while archers shot from behind tall shields held by their partners. Aramean tribesmen and armored slingers skirmished ahead, and provincial levies filled out the host. Cavalry rode in pairs, one man holding both horses' reins so the other could shoot with both hands, before learning to fight as lancers and horse archers in their own right. Above them all rode the king in his great four-horse chariot, beside the standards of the gods. The kings also waged war by terror, flaying rebels, impaling captives and deporting whole peoples, and they boasted of it in their inscriptions.

On the table, Assyria is a professional army that wins by grinding. Most of its regulars have Training 6 or better, so plant the Ṣāb Qašti behind their pavises and shoot, and let the Qurādū hold the line. The Kiṣir Šarrūti and the Ša Šēpē Retinue strike fear as much as wounds, so commit them where the enemy's morale is thinnest. Keep the Gurrāyū near your conscripts and Itu'ean archers, whom they kept in the fight. The Mugerri Šarri Retinue, the king's chariot, carries the army's Spearhead: when it charges, everything near it charges harder. Assyria has no light cavalry of its own, so take Adê if you need Arab camel riders or Scythian horse archers to cover your flanks.
