c. 550–330 BC · From Cyrus the Great to Darius III

When Cyrus the Great overthrew the Medes in 550 BC, he began the largest empire the world had yet seen. Within a generation the Achaemenid kings ruled from the Aegean to the Indus, and their armies drew on every people they governed: Persians and Medes at the core, with Bactrians, Saka nomads, Ionian Greeks, Egyptians and Indians called up from the satrapies.

The heart of the Persian battle line was the sparabara, the "shield bearers". A front rank of men with tall wicker shields, the spara, planted them to form a wall, while the ranks behind loosed volley after volley over their heads. The plan was to break the enemy with arrows before the lines ever met, then to finish them with cavalry. The Anušiyā, the Ten Thousand "Immortals" whose ranks were always kept full, were the king's elite foot, and Persian and Median horsemen, together with Saka horse archers, guarded the flanks and harried the enemy. By the fourth century the empire also fielded Kardakā, heavier infantry that may have been raised to answer the Greek hoplites who had beaten Persian foot at Marathon and Plataea, as well as scythed chariots and the occasional war elephant, as at Gaugamela in 331 BC.

On the table, Persia wins the shooting war. Plant the spara, darken the sky, and let the enemy come to you through the arrows; use your fast cavalry and chariots to pick the moment, and avoid trading blows with heavy infantry on equal terms.
