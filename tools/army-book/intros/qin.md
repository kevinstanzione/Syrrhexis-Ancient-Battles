c. 300–206 BC · From the wars of King Zhaoxiang to the fall of the First Emperor's dynasty

In the last century of the Warring States, seven kingdoms fought for China, and Qin, on the western frontier, outlasted them all. The reforms of Lord Shang had turned it into a state built for war: hereditary privilege gave way to ranks earned by taking enemy heads, households were bound together in groups of five that answered for one another, and every weapon came from state workshops to a single standard. Generals like Bai Qi destroyed whole armies, most famously at Changping in 260 BC, and in 221 BC King Zheng conquered the last of his rivals and named himself Qin Shi Huang, the First Emperor. He linked the northern walls, ran straight roads across the empire, and was buried with an army of terracotta soldiers. His dynasty collapsed only fifteen years later.

That terracotta army shows how Qin fought. Unarmored archers stood at the front as a screen. Behind them came columns of armored infantry with dagger-axes and halberds, blocks of crossbowmen kneeling and standing in turn, chariots with a driver, an archer and a halberdier, and cavalry who rode with a crossbow in one hand. In the command post stood a single chariot surrounded by its guard, and officers' chariots carried the drum that sent the army forward and the bell that called it back. When rebels marched on the capital in 209 BC, the general Zhang Han armed the convicts who had been building the emperor's tomb and sent them to fight.

On the table, Qin is a technical army, and every unit has one job. The Krap Tsut stop cavalry and chariots but should not be fed into a straight fight with enemy spearmen. The crossbows hit harder than any other missile troops in the game, but Braced Shot means they must stand still to shoot well, and the Kwat Trang have no melee weapons at all, so keep the Dzen Pong out in front and fall back behind your line when the enemy closes. Take two retinues if you can and use Drums and Gongs to run the two halves of your army separately. Send the Geng Da into a fight on their own to earn their Pardon, and save Changping for the turn your units have an enemy surrounded.
