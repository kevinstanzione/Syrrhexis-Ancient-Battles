c. 2600–2000 BC · From Early Dynastic Lagash to the Third Dynasty of Ur

The cities of Sumer, in the marshes and river plains of southern Mesopotamia, fought the first wars we can see in pictures. Lagash, Umma, Ur, Uruk and Kish were rival city-states, each ruled in the name of its god, and they fought for generations over fields, canals and water. Sargon of Akkad united them by force into the first empire. After Akkad fell, the kings of Ur rebuilt a Sumerian state, and even raised a long wall, the Muriq-Tidnim, to hold back the Amorite migrants, the Martu. It did not hold: Amorite pressure and an Elamite invasion brought Ur down around 2004 BC.

The Stele of the Vultures shows King Eannatum of Lagash leading a dense block of spearmen behind a wall of great rectangular shields, while the god Ningirsu holds the enemies of Lagash in his battle net. The Standard of Ur shows caped infantry with spears, and heavy four-wheeled battle wagons drawn by teams of kunga, donkey-onager hybrids, riding down the enemy. Small copper models show a lighter car as well, a two-wheeled straddle car whose driver sat astride it like a saddle.

On the table, Sumer wins with its wall. The Eren lock their guru, their shields, at the front of a deep block of spears, so lead with them, put them on Hold, and let Shields of Lagash carry the wall forward. The caped Šub-lugal and the king's Aga-ús follow them in. Use the fast Gigir-tur to goad enemy blocks out of line with Feign Retreat, then hold the heavy Gigir back until the enemy is engaged and crash into a flank. Keep the Martu in Ambush for the moment the line bends. Above all, protect your troops from enemy archers: almost nothing in this army carries armor.
