AD 1428–1521 · From the Triple Alliance to the fall of Tenochtitlan

In 1428 the Mexica of Tenochtitlan joined with Texcoco and Tlacopan to overthrow their overlords, and within a century this Triple Alliance ruled most of central Mexico. Its island capital, set among the lakes and floating gardens of the Valley of Mexico, was one of the largest cities in the world when the Spanish arrived in 1519. Conquered cities paid tribute in cloth, food and warriors, and the empire went to war almost every year.

Every Mexica man was trained for war, and he rose through the ranks by taking captives rather than by killing. A youth who took his first prisoner earned the right to new clothing and a name. Those who took several could join the great warrior societies: the Eagle and Jaguar warriors, and the elite Shorn Ones, the cuāchicqueh, who swore never to retreat. Warriors wore quilted cotton armour and carried round shields and the mācuahuitl, a wooden club edged with obsidian blades that Spanish soldiers claimed could behead a horse. Archers, slingers and dart-throwers armed with the atlatl opened every battle. Some wars, the "flower wars", were fought by arrangement, simply so that both sides could take captives for sacrifice.

On the table, the Aztecs win with speed and aggression. They have no heavy infantry and little armour, but every unit can move fast and hit hard. Soften the enemy with your dart-throwers and slingers, then pick your moment and close in quickly with the mācuahuitl, before the enemy's heavier troops can bring their weight to bear.
